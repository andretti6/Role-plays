The Scenario
What's happening in the world of work
▼

AI is no longer a future idea — it is in the room. It writes, draws, diagnoses, decides, and manages. Some workers fear it will take their jobs. Others say it will free them from boring tasks and let them focus on what humans do best: empathy, creativity, and meaning.

For psychology, this raises a deeper question. Work is not only money — it is identity, purpose, and social belonging. If a machine can do your job, what happens to your sense of self? And if AI changes how we relate to our own effort, what does that do to motivation, competence, and self-worth?

Real World Example
A study that changed how we see AI at work
▼
📊 The GitHub Copilot Study · 2022
55% faster
GitHub ran a controlled experiment with software developers using its AI assistant, Copilot. Developers who used it finished tasks 55% faster than those who did not.

But what caught psychologists' attention was something else: many developers reported that AI removed the frustrating, repetitive parts of coding, leaving more time for creative problem-solving.

In other words, the tool didn't replace the human — it reshaped which parts of the work the human experienced.
🧠 Psychological insight
When AI takes over low-skill tasks, it can actually increase engagement — but only if people still feel competent, in control, and connected to what they do. Without those needs met, the same tool can produce anxiety and meaninglessness.
